# Overall Project Conclusion

Across the different dimensions analysed, the findings demonstrate that revenue performance cannot be adequately evaluated using a single performance metric. Rather, it reflects the combined relationship between revenue generated, sales volume, promotional discounts and revenue lost through returns.

The results reveal that higher sales volume does not necessarily translate into higher revenue, just as stronger promotional performance does not necessarily depend on higher discount levels. Similarly, the impact of returned revenue on net revenue contribution must be assessed relative to the total revenue generated. In some instances, relatively high returned revenue did not prevent an entity from achieving the highest net revenue contribution because its total revenue remained sufficiently high.

Furthermore, the findings show that revenue performance varies across products, customer categories, payment methods, salespeople, regions and years. This highlights the importance of evaluating business performance across multiple dimensions rather than relying on isolated metrics.

Overall, the analysis demonstrates the value of a multidimensional approach to sales performance evaluation, providing a broader understanding of the factors associated with revenue generation and net revenue contribution.
